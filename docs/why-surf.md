# Why Surf

> “Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic.” — [Arthur C. Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarke%27s_three_laws)

Imagine a world where fewer than 0.05% of people can meaningfully wield magical technology.

I don't mean AI, for once. This is *Harry Potter* — the best-selling book series of all time, worldwide.

- In the *Harry Potter* universe, a tiny fraction of people are born with innate magical talents.
- The lucky few are then whisked away to highly secretive and elite institutions.
- Magic is used pretty much everywhere for the blessed: everything from cooking to medical treatment.

It's a genuine technology of superabundance. It's such a shame that only 0.05% of the population get to use it!

When I look at the deepest users of AI, what I see is that *they are having so much fun* right now.

AI is like a wave that's fast, powerful and unpredictable. That can be a stressful experience. But the deepest AI users are having the time of their lives *learning to* *surf* these waves.

This is why I created **Surf** — because I think we should *all* get to experience what it's like to surf these waves and wield this magic technology.

Surf helps you build a way of working and learning that keeps adapting to the frontier, using these core ideas:

- **Everyone can learn to do remarkable things with AI.** Surf meets you wherever you are and takes you on a journey at the right pace for you.
- **Good learning means being ready for change.** Surf helps you focus on adaptability via experimentation, so that when a new model emerges and [dramatically changes best practice](https://claude.com/blog/the-new-rules-of-context-engineering-for-claude-5-generation-models), you're ready.
- **We all deserve to have these experiences.** Surf is a free and open-source add-on to the most popular AI products (ChatGPT and Claude, both by desktop app or CLI).

I hope you learn to Surf. Most of all, I hope you have fun doing so.

---

Footnote. Using *Harry Potter* as an analogy for AI is risky because it may trivialise serious social problems, since the series does not really examine what hyper-concentration of [wealth](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c9q29j47v9ro) and [power](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/economics-transformative-ai/ais-use-knowledge-society) might do to a society. I hope Surf can *also* play a part in reducing those risks by helping more people get deeply proficient with AI and agents — but my primary motivation is *personal empowerment* rather than *social conscience*.
