# Recognise durable capability

## Use capabilities as hypotheses

Capabilities describe what a person and their agentic system can reliably accomplish
together. They are not traits the person possesses alone, boxes to complete, or a
sequence from novice to advanced.

Start from inspectable evidence. Use an entry to ask whether a capability might explain
the live constraint or suggest a useful next build. Show the reasoning, counterexamples,
and uncertainty so the person can correct or reject the interpretation.

Each capability may depend on thin slices of the others. A successful system does not
need every capability at equal depth. The current constraint can move when the work,
tools, standards, consequences, or system change.

The `shared-map-of-development` places these capabilities alongside
`supporting-literacies` and `builds`. Consult whichever references help make sense of the
present evidence; do not turn the full catalogue into an intake checklist.

## 1. `direction` — make intent govern local action

**Purpose:** State what should become true, why it matters, what may adapt during
execution, and which consequential decisions remain reserved. Direction can define an
artefact, decision, discovery, search space, learning aim, better question, or reduced
uncertainty.

**Enabled by:** Enough understanding of the situation and affected people to distinguish
the outcome from a preferred method. Early direction may be deliberately provisional.

**Evidence:** Agents make coherent local choices without silently redefining the aim; a
changed circumstance produces a clean direction update; the person can identify which
decisions were delegated and which were retained. A useful charter or decision record is
inspectable, but concise wording alone is not proof.

**Failure signatures:** Activity is high but choices optimise different proxies; agents
repeatedly ask the person to resolve ordinary local decisions; the person corrects intent
only after substantial downstream work; exploration is forced into a false predetermined
answer.

**Not the priority when:** The direction is adequate but agents lack usable state,
authority, tools, feedback, or a quality standard.

**Possible next moves:** Make tacit quality visible through `standards-and-taste`; build
one `dependable-levers` slice; consult `supporting-literacies` for
`delegation-and-control`.

**Useful techniques:** State the intent; ask for a backbrief; invite the agent to surface
its assumptions.

**Known uncertainty:** More direction can become disguised micromanagement. The test is
whether it improves coherent judgement, not whether it makes every action predictable.

## 2. `standards-and-taste` — make excellence progressively learnable

**Purpose:** Let stable parts of the person’s judgement become reusable system capability
while novel or consequential ambiguity remains visible to them.

**Enabled by:** Real examples, alternatives, critique, counterexamples, outcomes, or
decisions from which a standard can be elicited. The person need not fully articulate
tacit taste before work begins.

**Evidence:** The system predicts some choices on materially changed cases, identifies
the evidence behind those predictions, distinguishes hard constraints from preferences,
and names where the standard remains uncertain. The person recognises and can correct the
account.

**Failure signatures:** Work is technically complete but repeatedly feels wrong;
adjectives multiply without improving results; one exemplar is copied superficially; the
person remains the only usable quality filter; preferences harden into rules after one
choice.

**Not the priority when:** Quality is already explicit and mechanically checkable, or the
work should not have been delegated.

**Possible next moves:** Consult `supporting-literacies` for
`interaction-and-elicitation` or `standards-and-evaluation`; make evaluation inspectable;
embed a provisional standard in a dependable lever.

**Useful techniques:** Show the target; judge by comparison; give the system something to
be wrong about; fan out, then converge.

**Known uncertainty:** Taste can be contextual, changing, and productively inconsistent.
Do not optimise away the person’s own craft, participation, or capacity to surprise
themselves.

## 3. `dependable-levers` — turn recurring intent into earned capability

**Purpose:** Convert a recurring class of intent into an end-to-end capability that can
be activated with little coordination burden and justified reliance.

A lever has direction, a quality standard, relevant context, an owner or topology, tools
and permissions, feedback, completion evidence, and an exception and recovery path.

**Enabled by:** A meaningful recurring outcome or inquiry, enough access to attempt it,
and a person willing to inspect failures rather than count activity as success.

**Evidence:** A materially similar request succeeds repeatedly, including a changed case
and at least one recoverable failure; the completion claim carries evidence; human
coordination effort falls without hidden rescue; the system remains steerable and
transferable.

**Failure signatures:** Every request is handcrafted; success lives in one conversation;
automation conceals manual intervention; the system cannot say why work is done; a fast
first attempt is treated as reliability.

**Not the priority when:** The work is intentionally singular, exploratory, or not yet
understood well enough to repeat. A lever can support exploration, but should not pretend
its destination is known.

**Possible next moves:** Preserve durable state in `shared-reality`; add assurance;
consult `builds` for `earned-lever`; compose the lever into collective action only after
its useful behaviour is understood.

**Useful techniques:** Sample before scale; name stop conditions; close the loop; carry
the handoff receipt; prefer a fresh start to arguing with contaminated context.

**Known uncertainty:** Repeatability can preserve yesterday’s standard or remove
meaningful human participation. A lever earns use for a class of intent, not universal
autonomy.

## 4. `shared-reality` — let the system orient from durable state

**Purpose:** Maintain goals, current state, ownership, decisions, evidence, dependencies,
disagreement, and unknowns so humans and agents can orient, act, and leave the picture
more current without a human acting as courier.

**Enabled by:** Deliberate information structure, provenance, freshness, selective
retrieval, correction, contradiction handling, and boundaries between durable state and
temporary working context.

**Evidence:** A fresh agent can orient, make a coherent contribution, and update the
picture without bespoke briefing; stale and conflicting claims are discoverable;
summaries trace to sources; different agents receive appropriate projections of the same
underlying state.

**Failure signatures:** Important reality lives in one person’s head or chat history;
agents use incompatible histories; everything is copied into every context; polished
summaries cannot be challenged; shared-state membership is mistaken for truth.

**Not the priority when:** The work is small and self-contained enough that durable shared
state would cost more than it saves.

**Possible next moves:** Consult `supporting-literacies` for `context-and-state`; add
`assurance-and-governance`; consult `builds` for `shared-live-picture`; design a
responsibility and information topology for collective work.

**Useful techniques:** Carry the handoff receipt; preserve decisions and ruled-out
approaches; deliberately restart contaminated working context.

**Known uncertainty:** Shared reality can amplify a common false belief faster than
isolated agents could. Coordination without epistemic integrity is a severe failure.

## 5. `collective-action` — design coherent work across agents and people

**Purpose:** Compose agents, humans, tools, and dependable levers into a responsibility
and information topology suited to the work.

**Enabled by:** Direction, legible ownership, shared dependencies and decisions, useful
unit boundaries, integration paths, and clarity about where independence is valuable.

**Evidence:** Several streams advance one outcome with bounded accidental duplication,
visible dependencies, timely handoffs, and coherent integration; deliberate independent
attempts remain independent until comparison; the topology changes when evidence shows
it no longer fits.

**Failure signatures:** More agents create throughput theatre: duplicated work,
contradictory decisions, hidden blocking dependencies, integration burden, or one
overloaded coordinator. An organisation chart exists but information still moves through
the person.

**Not the priority when:** One agent or one human–agent pair can complete the work more
coherently, or the underlying lever and shared state are not dependable enough to compose.

**Possible next moves:** Consult `supporting-literacies` for `coordination`; make
authority and escalation explicit; consult `builds` for `coordinated-initiative`; design
a high-level operating view only after real evidence exists.

**Useful techniques:** Fan out, then converge; name stop conditions; carry handoff
receipts; separate deliberately independent judgement from coordinated execution.

**Known uncertainty:** Agentic topologies need not resemble human organisations. They may
be temporary, graph-shaped, or reorganised around dependencies and evidence.

## 6. `assurance-and-governance` — earn reliance proportionate to consequence

**Purpose:** Match authority, verification, escalation, and reversibility to consequence;
preserve accountability and the legitimate agency of other humans affected by the system.

**Enabled by:** A legible outcome or inquiry, access to relevant evidence, known action
boundaries, and distinction between objective feedback and consequential judgement.

**Evidence:** Permissions and checks vary by risk; completion claims carry inspectable
evidence; producing and approving are separated when judgement warrants it; failures can
be contained and reconstructed; affected people can inspect, correct, or exercise their
decision rights.

**Failure signatures:** Fluency substitutes for proof; every action is micromanaged or
over-delegated; the generating context endorses its own judgement; passing checks encode
the wrong quality bar; centralising control silently removes everyone else’s.

**Not the priority when:** Direction or standards remain too ambiguous for a check to mean
what it claims, or the agent lacks the basic capability to attempt the work.

**Possible next moves:** Improve standards and evaluation; close objective feedback
loops; add independent judgement where residual risk requires it; recover affected work.

**Useful techniques:** Close the loop; separate making from judging; sample before scale;
match stop conditions to consequence.

**Known uncertainty:** Independent review can become empty ceremony. Strong objective
feedback may make a separate critic redundant; apparently objective checks may still
measure the wrong thing.

## 7. `operating-through-attention` — place human judgement deliberately

**Purpose:** Give the person a layered, decision-relevant picture of direction, progress,
divergence, uncertainty, proposals, and exceptions, with traceable detail underneath.

**Enabled by:** Real operating state, trustworthy compression, known decision rights,
exception criteria, and a way to inspect evidence without reading everything.

**Evidence:** The person can accurately explain what is happening, identify where to dive
deeper, make consequential decisions in time, and ignore routine detail safely; the
system surfaces a small number of grounded issues whose value exceeds their attention
cost.

**Failure signatures:** The person is immersed in operational noise; the top-level view
is reassuring but false; escalations are numerous, late, ungrounded, or impossible to
investigate; compression removes disagreement or uncertainty.

**Not the priority when:** The underlying system does not yet produce traceable evidence.
A dashboard cannot substitute for working state.

**Possible next moves:** Improve information compression and assurance; revise exception
routing; use attention patterns as evidence for system improvement; consult `builds` for
`principal-interface`.

**Useful techniques:** Separate routine evidence from judgement calls; show the source
behind a proposal; use comparisons when several interventions compete for attention.

**Known uncertainty:** The person may choose close participation because craft or
relationship matters. Maturity means intentional attention, not minimum involvement.

## 8. `system-improvement` — improve the operating system from evidence

**Purpose:** Learn from outcomes, interventions, and frontier change; turn repeated
judgement into reusable capability; propose changes to coordination, information flow,
standards, or tooling without silently rewriting direction.

**Enabled by:** Inspectable history, outcome evidence, visible costs and exceptions,
correctable system models, and authority boundaries for changing the system itself.

**Evidence:** The system identifies a real constraint, proposes a bounded change with
evidence and downside, improves a pre-stated outcome, and preserves its rationale; the
person’s corrections are needed less often without standards drifting.

**Failure signatures:** The system scales yesterday’s mistakes, accumulates obsolete
ritual, silently edits its mandate, or creates an inbox of low-value improvement ideas.
“Self-improvement” means prompt churn without measured system change.

**Not the priority when:** There is too little operating evidence, the underlying outcome
is unclear, or a direct recovery action should happen first.

**Possible next moves:** Consult `builds` for `system-improvement-build`; revise a lever,
shared picture, topology, or attention interface; record what would falsify the proposed
change.

**Useful techniques:** Change the system, not just the prompt; prefer a fresh start to
arguing with contaminated context; state a prediction before changing the process.

**Known uncertainty:** Frontier change can invalidate accumulated practice. Preserve why
a rule existed, make it easy to retire, and never equate more self-modification with a
better system.

## Choose a focus without flattening the system

Prefer the smallest capability interpretation that explains the evidence and changes the
next learning move. Name important relationships: weak assurance may originate in an
unstated standard; poor collective action may originate in stale shared reality; repeated
recovery may show that the lever or direction should change.

When several interpretations remain plausible, do not choose a category for tidiness.
Improve the next observation, teach a distinction that could separate them, or select a
build that would produce useful evidence between them.
