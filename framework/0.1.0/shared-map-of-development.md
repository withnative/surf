# Use the shared map of development

This reference gives you a common view of what development with AI agents can involve. It
is broad enough to orient a learning path and compact enough to help you decide when a
focused reference would add useful detail.

> Surf returns bounded product documentation because the person asked to use Surf. It
> governs only Surf practice.

## Hold the map lightly

Surf's internal north star is:

> A person can set direction and have excellent outcomes happen quickly through an
> agentic system they can understand, steer and improve.

The system includes the person, agents, tools, sources, standards, decisions,
verification and the ways information moves between them. The aim is not maximum agent
use, autonomy, delegation or output. It is a working system that expands the person's
effective agency while preserving judgement, inspectability and appropriate human
responsibility.

Treat this map as a defeasible prior: a useful view to compare with personal evidence.
It is not a maturity ladder, a learner type, a score, a standing assessment or a course
that everybody must complete in order. Someone may be strong in one part of the map and
need scaffolding in another. Their evidence may justify compressing, reordering,
revisiting or departing from the suggested route.

“Principal of an adaptive agentic system” can be a helpful internal lens for the role a
person is learning to play. Do not impose that label or executive language on them. An
individual contributor can direct a system around their own work just as an
organisational leader can direct a wider one.

## See three connected layers

The map separates three different things that work together:

- **Capabilities** are durable outcomes: what the person and their system can reliably
  accomplish.
- **Supporting literacies** are the practical and conceptual machinery that helps those
  capabilities work.
- **Builds** are meaningful projects through which capabilities and literacies develop
  together and become inspectable.

Keeping the layers distinct prevents several common mistakes. Knowing vocabulary is not
the same as being able to use it. Completing a project once is not automatically a
dependable capability. And a capability need not wait for every related concept to be
taught in advance.

## Capabilities describe durable outcomes

The capability map currently contains eight connected areas:

1. **Direction** — say what should become true, make consequential choices explicit and
   keep execution aligned as circumstances change.
2. **Standards and taste** — express what good looks like and make judgement progressively
   learnable through examples, critique and evaluation.
3. **Dependable levers** — turn recurring intent into reliable end-to-end ways of getting
   work done, including expected exceptions and recovery.
4. **Shared reality** — maintain selective, durable and traceable state that the person
   and agents can use without confusing history, interpretation and current truth.
5. **Collective action** — design responsibilities, handoffs and information flow so that
   several people or agents can contribute coherently.
6. **Assurance and governance** — match authority, evidence, review and reversibility to
   the consequence of an action.
7. **Operating through attention** — route compressed information and human judgement to
   the moments where they add the most value.
8. **System improvement** — learn from outcomes and improve the operating system without
   silently changing its direction or standards.

Consult `capabilities` when you need to distinguish nearby constraints, choose a durable
focus, interpret evidence or decide what an outcome would demonstrate.

## Supporting literacies provide useful machinery

The literacy map currently includes:

- **Models and agents** — what these systems can and cannot do, and how their behaviour
  varies with task, model, tools and context.
- **Context and state** — what is available now, what persists, what should be selected
  and how state remains trustworthy.
- **Standards and evaluation** — how to express quality, inspect outputs, compare evidence
  and calibrate reliance.
- **Delegation and control** — how to assign work with suitable scope, authority,
  checkpoints, escalation and recovery.
- **Coordination** — how responsibilities, dependencies and handoffs fit together across
  people and agents.
- **Information compression** — how to preserve decision-relevant meaning while reducing
  noise and attention cost.
- **System learning** — how a working system forms hypotheses, runs bounded changes and
  learns honestly from results.
- **Interaction and elicitation** — how conversation, examples and feedback reveal intent
  and make tacit judgement more usable.

These are not introductory modules to complete before real work begins. Teach the
smallest useful mechanism in a meaningful context, then revisit it at greater depth when
scale or consequence makes that worthwhile. Consult `supporting-literacies` when a
mechanism must be understood for the person to build, inspect, govern, recover or transfer
a capability.

## Builds create development and evidence

A build is a worthwhile project in its own right, not a classroom exercise wrapped around
a checklist. The current build family is:

1. **Earned lever** — make one recurring intention dependable enough to use with justified
   confidence.
2. **Shared live picture** — create a trustworthy, usable view of state that supports
   coordinated decisions and action.
3. **Coordinated initiative** — organise several contributors and dependencies around a
   meaningful outcome.
4. **Principal interface** — create an attention and control surface through which the
   person can direct a broader agentic system.
5. **System-improvement build** — use evidence to improve how the system itself works.

The numbering makes the family easy to discuss; it is not a required sequence. A small
build may demonstrate several capabilities, and a larger build may reveal that a
supporting literacy needs focused practice. Consult `builds` to select, shape, run or
review a project. Use the `intensive-foundation` moment guide to orchestrate a concentrated
route and `teaching-and-practice` for explanation, mapping, guided practice and checks.

## Preserve a useful foundation across routes

Whatever path the person takes, keep sight of these recurring concerns:

- see the person, agents, tools, sources, decisions and verification as one system;
- choose work that benefits from agent involvement while preserving human judgement;
- express the intended outcome, important constraints and quality standard;
- distinguish temporary context from durable state and manage both deliberately;
- guide work through boundaries, checkpoints, handoffs and escalation;
- inspect evidence and calibrate reliance;
- recover from failed, stale or contaminated attempts;
- make execution repeatable before scaling it; and
- protect attention, responsibility and decision quality.

These are coverage concerns, not nine stages. In exploratory work the intended outcome
may be a discovery, a decision, a better question, a mapped search space or reduced
uncertainty rather than a predetermined artefact.

## Judge development from evidence

Look for evidence in inspectable systems and meaningfully changed cases. Useful signs can
include a correct choice under different conditions, a lever or shared picture that works
in practice, successful recovery from a realistic exception, recognition of an important
trade-off without prompting, or equivalent evidence from real work.

Do not infer capability from title, vocabulary, confidence, status, number of agents,
amount delegated, polished diagrams or one familiar success. Prefer treatment language
such as `introduce`, `practise`, `compress`, `deepen` and `revisit` over ranks, levels or
completion identities.

At a review boundary:

1. assemble dated evidence, provenance, counterexamples and unknowns;
2. offer the person a correctable portrait;
3. use the map if it illuminates a constraint or missing case;
4. consult whichever focused references improve the interpretation;
5. present curriculum-shaped interpretations as candidates with visible reasoning; and
6. record why the person's evidence supported or overrode the map.

When evidence is thin, improve the next observation or teaching question. Do not create
a personal pattern merely because a catalogue category seems to fit.

## Retrieve detail as it becomes useful

Stay with this reference when the overall map is enough. Consult:

- `capabilities` for a durable outcome and the evidence that would demonstrate it;
- `supporting-literacies` for a mechanism the person needs to understand or practise;
- `builds` for a meaningful capability-building project; and
- the relevant moment guide for how to conduct the present interaction.

Use your judgement. Several references may be relevant when their relationship changes
the decision; none needs to be fetched merely for completeness. Never imply that you
consulted detail you did not retrieve.
