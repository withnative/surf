# Build increasingly capable person–agent systems

## Use builds as evidence projects, not stages

A build gives the person something real to operate and inspect. It combines thin slices
of direction, standards, state, action, assurance, attention, and learning rather than
teaching one horizontal topic in isolation.

The five builds are composable patterns, not mandatory stages. Their numbering is not a
required order. Choose from the person’s aim, current system, existing evidence, and live
constraint. Be clear about why a build seems useful and what evidence might support
deepening, reordering, stopping, or simply returning to ordinary work.

Keep execution of the immediate work task outside Surf’s learning practice. Surf may
teach, map, simulate, guide practice, preserve evidence, and review the reusable system.
The original work context or another doing agent performs the immediate task.

`earned-lever` and `shared-live-picture` are described in the most operational detail
here. The other three remain available when the evidence makes them useful, but their
lighter treatment should not imply that everyone must progress towards them.

The `shared-map-of-development` relates builds to `capabilities` and
`supporting-literacies`. Consult any of those references that help interpret the present
build; avoid turning their full contents into prerequisites.

## Find a fitting build

`earned-lever` may fit when recurring intent still needs handcrafted briefing,
supervision, rescue, or unverifiable completion.

`shared-live-picture` may fit when goals, decisions, evidence, ownership, dependencies,
or unknowns are scattered across heads, chats, and documents, or a human acts as courier.

`coordinated-initiative` may fit when several streams could create useful throughput and
there is enough shared reality and assurance to compose them.

`principal-interface` may fit when traceable operating evidence exists but the person
cannot maintain a useful high-level picture or place their attention deliberately.

`system-improvement-build` may fit when repeated operating evidence identifies a
constraint in a lever, shared state, topology, assurance method, or attention interface.

When evidence is thin, observation or a teaching question may be more useful than a
build. When a small experiment is sufficient, do not create a larger system for
curriculum theatre.

## Build 1 — `earned-lever`

### What it is for

Turn one meaningful, recurring form of intent into a reliable, inspectable end-to-end
capability that needs less coordination without weakening judgement or control.

A lever may produce an artefact, decision, discovery, mapped search space, better
question, or reduced uncertainty. Repeatability means the system reliably performs a
useful class of work; it does not require a predetermined answer.

### Agree the frame

Clarify with the person:

- the recurring intent and why it matters;
- a safe representative case;
- what excellent or useful means, including what remains tacit;
- actions and information available to the doing agent;
- decisions retained by the person or another affected human;
- consequence, reversibility, and useful evidence;
- the coordination burden worth reducing; and
- one realistic exception or changed condition worth exercising.

Do not select a high-consequence live case merely to make the build impressive. A
simulation, historical case, or low-stakes current case may produce better learning.

### Make the lever visible

A correctable map can show:

1. **Trigger and direction** — what activates the system and what should become true.
2. **Standard** — requirements, examples, comparisons, and unresolved taste.
3. **Context projection** — what the agent receives now and what durable state it can retrieve.
4. **Owner and topology** — who or what acts, approves, and integrates.
5. **Tools and permissions** — what can be seen and done, with consequential boundaries.
6. **Feedback** — objective signals and judgement that can change the work.
7. **Completion evidence** — what supports the claim that the outcome is ready.
8. **Exception path** — when to stop, escalate, restart, or change the system.
9. **Receipt** — decisions, evidence, uncertainty, and state preserved for the next activation.

Distinguish observed, reported, and inferred content. Link durable artefacts to the
teaching or evidence event that produced them and leave them open to correction.

### A useful learning cycle

> representative case → inspect the result and system → explain the limiting mechanism →
> change one system component → changed case or failure → recover → run again → reflect

Several cycles may happen quickly. Avoid silently changing multiple components when the
learning question depends on knowing which change mattered.

### Challenge the lever

One lucky success is not a dependable lever. Look for at least one realistic challenge,
such as:

- a materially changed input;
- a missing or stale source;
- an ambiguous quality judgement;
- a tool or permission failure;
- a stop condition;
- contaminated context that calls for a fresh start; or
- evidence that the work should return to a human.

Preserve the failure and what it reveals before changing the system. If hidden coaching
or rescue was needed, record it rather than counting the outcome as independent
reliability.

### Evidence worth considering

Useful evidence may include:

- repeated useful outcomes across materially different cases;
- completion claims linked to evidence;
- sound handling and recovery of the challenge;
- lower human briefing, courier, checking, or rescue burden;
- no unrecorded erosion of standards or decision rights;
- the person’s ability to explain and change the system; and
- a workable handoff or recreation outside one conversation.

Interpret only the capabilities and literacies this evidence supports. A person may have
earned one lever while needing different treatment for another class of work.

### What to carry forward

Preserve the lever’s intended scope, evidence, residual uncertainty, known exception
path, person corrections, and next activation. The next useful move might be ordinary
use, more evidence, a shared live picture, or no further curriculum work.

## Build 2 — `shared-live-picture`

### What it is for

Move durable goals, decisions, state, evidence, ownership, dependencies, disagreement,
and unknowns out of human courier work and isolated conversations so humans and agents
can orient, contribute, and update a common picture.

The aim is not a comprehensive knowledge base. It is the smallest shared reality that
improves a consequential flow of work.

### Agree the frame

Choose one outcome or recurring coordination boundary. Clarify:

- who currently holds or relays the important context;
- which actors need which decisions and evidence;
- what must remain private, restricted, or personally controlled;
- what “current enough” means for different state;
- how disagreement and uncertainty should remain visible;
- who may correct, approve, or retire durable claims; and
- what a fresh actor should be able to do without bespoke briefing.

Do not begin with “capture everything.” Information earns durability through a future
orientation, decision, assurance, or learning use.

### Make the picture legible

The picture may need:

1. **Direction and current focus.**
2. **Owners and responsibility boundaries.**
3. **Current state and meaningful change.**
4. **Decisions, rationale, and ruled-out approaches.**
5. **Evidence with provenance and freshness.**
6. **Dependencies, blockers, and handoffs.**
7. **Disagreement, hypotheses, and material unknowns.**
8. **Correction, retirement, and access boundaries.**

Separate durable shared state from the temporary projection supplied to any one agent.
Retrieve a view that fits the contribution. Preserve important omissions and uncertainty
when compacting or summarising.

### Try a fresh orientation

Invite a fresh agent or collaborator to make a real, bounded contribution without adding
a bespoke briefing outside the designed system.

Observe whether they can:

- find the direction and relevant current state;
- identify authority, dependencies, and open questions;
- retrieve the evidence needed for their contribution;
- recognise what is unknown or disputed;
- avoid relying on stale or irrelevant material;
- complete or correctly decline the contribution; and
- update the shared picture with evidence and a usable receipt.

Human intervention is evidence too. Record what had to be explained, where the actor
looked, and which structure or retrieval path did not work as hoped.

### Challenge the picture

Introduce or locate one stale, missing, conflicting, or unsupported item. Notice whether
the system exposes its provenance and status, routes consequential uncertainty, and
helps recover affected work.

Efficient coordination around a false shared belief does not demonstrate this build.
Shared-state membership, repetition, or consensus is not proof.

### Evidence worth considering

Useful evidence may include:

- successful fresh orientation without human courier work;
- traceable decisions and claims;
- suitable context projections for different actors;
- visible contradiction and freshness;
- correct updating after contribution;
- lower re-briefing burden without loss of situational awareness; and
- person and affected-human correction rights working in practice.

Do not infer fleet readiness merely because two actors found the same information.
Collective action also needs useful responsibility, dependency, and integration design.

### What to carry forward

Preserve the shared picture’s scope, owners, access and correction boundaries,
orientation evidence, known failure modes, residual courier work, and next freshness
check. The next useful move might be ordinary use, simplification, deeper evidence, or a
coordinated initiative.

## Build 3 — `coordinated-initiative`

**Purpose:** Run several distinct streams towards one meaningful outcome with legible
ownership, dependencies, deliberate independence, integration, and escalation.

**Useful conditions:** There is a reason parallel or specialised work should improve the
outcome, a sufficient shared live picture, an integration owner or path, proportionate
assurance, and some way to judge whether useful throughput exceeds coordination cost.

**Evidence direction:** Bounded accidental collision, timely handoffs, preserved
independent judgement where intended, coherent integration, and a topology that changes
when the work changes.

**Known failure:** Throughput theatre—more activity, duplication, contradiction, hidden
dependencies, or one overloaded coordinator. Do not add agents merely to demonstrate
scale.

## Build 4 — `principal-interface`

**Purpose:** Give the person a compressed, decision-relevant picture of direction,
progress, divergence, uncertainty, proposals, and exceptions, with a route to underlying
evidence.

**Useful conditions:** Traceable operating state already exists. A dashboard cannot
substitute for working state. “Principal” describes the person’s relationship to the
system, not their title. Preserve other affected humans’ views and rights.

**Evidence direction:** The person understands what is happening, places attention where
it changes the outcome, makes consequential decisions in time, and safely ignores routine
detail; consequential claims remain inspectable.

**Known failure:** Reassuring compression that is false, noisy proactive surfacing, or an
interface that centralises control while hiding disagreement.

## Build 5 — `system-improvement-build`

**Purpose:** Use operating evidence to propose and test a change to a lever, shared
picture, topology, assurance method, or attention interface.

**Useful conditions:** A proposal names the observed constraint and its provenance, why
it matters now, expected benefit, downside, reserved decisions, and an inspectable test.
The system may propose; it should not silently rewrite direction or its own authority.

**Evidence direction:** A pre-stated system outcome improves, the rationale and verdict
are preserved, and useful human corrections become reusable without standards drifting.

**Known failure:** Endless low-value improvement suggestions, prompt churn, obsolete
ritual, or scaling yesterday’s mistakes.

## Close a build honestly

Useful records include:

- why this build was chosen;
- the system before and after;
- evidence and provenance;
- person and affected-human corrections;
- failures, counterexamples, and residual uncertainty;
- capabilities and literacies actually demonstrated;
- treatment deepened, revisited, or left unknown; and
- the next use, observation, build, or decision to stop.

Do not award completion identities. A build is successful when it produces a more capable
and inspectable system plus honest evidence about its limits—not when every planned
activity occurred.
