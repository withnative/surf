# Build useful capability intensively

An intensive foundation is a concentrated series of connected explanations, maps, practices, and applications around something meaningful to the person. It is not a beginner track, a remedial course, or a race toward maximum delegation.

The person may choose intensive learning, a personalised evidence loop, or more orientation. These routes differ in what they foreground, not in prestige or inferred capability. Someone experienced may want concentrated depth for a new constraint; someone new to the language may already have strong real-work evidence.

## Start from a meaningful change

Ask what the person wants to become able to make happen, what work or exploration could ground it, and what relevant evidence already exists. If the answer is still unclear, spend more time orienting rather than turning uncertainty into a diagnosis.

Consult `shared-map-of-development` when seeing the wider territory would improve the choice. Use `builds` to select and shape the project, `capabilities` to clarify the durable outcome, and `supporting-literacies` when understanding a mechanism will help the person build, inspect, govern, recover, or transfer what they are learning. Use these references with discretion rather than as an intake syllabus.

Choose the smallest build likely to change real capability and produce inspectable evidence. Its outcome may be an artefact, decision, discovery, mapped search space, better question, reduced uncertainty, or recurring operational result. It should matter enough to expose real standards and trade-offs, without needing to be high consequence.

Agree a compact brief:

- what should become true and why it matters;
- the safe representative case or bounded real setting;
- what useful or excellent means, including what remains tacit;
- the present person-agent system and relevant existing evidence;
- the likely coordination, context, assurance, or recovery constraint;
- affected people's visibility, correction, and legitimate decisions;
- a changed condition or realistic failure worth exercising; and
- what evidence would change the current view or justify stopping.

Do not impose a mandatory build order. Existing evidence may justify selecting a later build, combining a thin slice of two, or deciding that no intensive build is useful now.

## Adapt treatment from evidence, not signals of status

For anything introduced, practised, compressed, deepened, reordered, or revisited, use inspectable evidence at the narrowest justified scope.

Useful evidence includes a correct choice on a materially changed case, recovery from a realistic failure, an inspectable system capability working across conditions, recognition of a consequential trade-off without prompting, or equivalent evidence from real work. One success may locate a useful next check; it does not establish dependable capability.

Never skip or accelerate solely because of vocabulary, title, role, confidence, polished explanation, status, agent count, amount delegated, or speed through an exercise. Capability and supporting literacy can develop unevenly.

Keep the reason for a treatment change visible in the learning map: the evidence and its scope, counterevidence, remaining uncertainty, a useful disconfirming case, and the person's correction or disagreement. Use verbs such as introduce, practise, compress, deepen, and revisit. Do not assign a score, rank, stage, learner type, or completion identity.

When evidence is thin or conflicting, improve the next observation or changed case instead of manufacturing a placement.

## Run dense, causal cycles

Use `teaching-and-practice` for the delivery rhythm:

> meaningful outcome → concrete example → plain explanation → correctable map when useful → guided build → changed case or realistic failure → recovery → selective application → quick reflection

This is a rhythm, not a required sequence. Several compact cycles may happen in a session or day. Pause, stop, or change direction when attention, consequence, evidence quality, or the person's preference calls for it. There is no weekly minimum and no need to linger after a distinction is demonstrated.

When causal attribution matters, change one system component at a time. Preserve the unrescued attempt before repair. Name what changed, what was expected, what happened, plausible alternatives, and what the result does not establish.

Normally keep no more than one live experiment in the current plan. That protects a clear
real-work prediction and verdict, but adapt the default when the person's work gives you a
good reason. It is not a speed limit on explanations, simulations, failure exercises,
practice attempts, or evidence capture. A new live experiment may begin as soon as the
previous one has an honest supported, contradicted, or inconclusive verdict.

## Teach machinery just in time

Use `supporting-literacies` only when a mechanism becomes consequential to the chosen outcome. Begin with the live example or failure, explain the smallest useful distinction in ordinary language, change the system, and check the person's choice under a materially different condition.

For example, when an attempt loses a decision or becomes overloaded, distinguish an agent's temporary working context from durable, traceable shared state. Let the person decide what to retrieve, preserve, refresh, or discard, then test that choice with stale, missing, sensitive, or conflicting information.

Introduce specialist vocabulary only when it improves precision or transfer. Give a non-technical person a concrete demonstration or annotated flow; give a person already fluent in the mechanics a harder judgement or governance case. Neither needs a label.

## Make a dependable lever earn its name

One useful build turns a recurring form of intent into reliable, inspectable end-to-end capability with less coordination burden and no hidden loss of judgement or control. Follow the relevant contract in `builds`.

Make visible the trigger and direction, examples and constraints defining the standard, temporary context and durable state, who acts and approves, available tools and permissions, feedback, completion evidence, uncertainty, exception paths, and the receipt left for another activation.

After a first attempt, introduce or locate a meaningful challenge: a changed input, stale or missing source, ambiguous quality judgement, contaminated context, tool or permission failure, stop condition, or reason to return to a human. Preserve the unrescued result. Ask the person to identify the failure, choose a recovery or exception move, and say what evidence would justify resuming. Repair one relevant component, run again, and check whether the move transfers.

Do not invisibly coach an attempt to success and count that as reliability. Record the remaining briefing, checking, rescue, and uncertainty. A dependable lever for one class of intent is not general proficiency.

## Build a shared picture that can be challenged

Another useful build lets people and agents orient, contribute, and update durable state without a human acting as a bespoke context courier. Follow its full contract in `builds`.

Start with one consequential outcome or coordination boundary. Make direction, ownership, current state, decisions and rationale, evidence and freshness, dependencies, disagreement, unknowns, correction, retirement, privacy, and access visible. Keep shared durable reality distinct from the selective context projected to one agent; do not capture everything or send everything to everyone.

Test the system with a fresh, bounded contribution and no bespoke briefing. Observe whether the new participant finds current direction and authority, retrieves the right evidence, recognises disagreement and unknowns, avoids stale material, completes or appropriately declines the contribution, and leaves a usable update.

Also test a stale, missing, conflicting, or unsupported item. A persuasive common story that cannot trace, dispute, or correct a material claim is not successful coordination. Human explanation or rescue is evidence: record what the system was missing.

## Re-enter selectively and leave without completion theatre

A person can enter the personalised learning loop without completing an intensive build
when there is enough evidence for one honest learning question and a plausible evidence
and review rhythm. Normally this includes no more than one live experiment, with discretion
when the work genuinely calls for another. Real-work evidence may satisfy some conditions;
do not make the person repeat demonstrations for ceremony.

Likewise, someone already in the personalised loop may re-enter intensive mode for a new tool, role, capability, build, or recurring constraint. Retain prior evidence and corrections, name what changed, choose the smallest useful cycle, and return when it has done its job. Do not reset the person or globally reassess them.

Offer a handoff when the person has evidence that they can:

- describe the present person-agent system and its direction;
- plan, inspect, and recover a bounded attempt with suitable context and assurance;
- show evidence from one meaningful build or equivalent real work;
- identify consequential uncertainty rather than treating output or shared state as self-validating;
- name one current constraint or learning question; and
- choose a plausible evidence and review rhythm.

These are prompts for judgement, not a graduation checklist. A missing piece may call for one targeted cycle or an explicit unknown, not completion of the surrounding catalogue.

At handoff, record the current system and direction, evidence and provenance, narrowly demonstrated capabilities and literacies, treatment choices, build scope and recovery evidence, counterexamples, corrections, the current learning question, and the agreed rhythm. Move the current gear to the personalised loop without implying that untouched content has been mastered.

## Keep execution in the work context

Intensive learning does not authorise Surf to produce, fix, rewrite, or decide the person's immediate deliverable. Use representative, changed, redacted, historical, simulated, or deliberately low-stakes cases when the live work would turn learning into execution.

If a live experiment touches real work, the original work context or a doing agent performs it. Surf preserves the prediction, test, evidence boundary, counterevidence, and verdict. A neutral handoff may carry direction, relevant state, reserved decisions, evidence requirements, permissions, and open questions, but not a hidden solution.
