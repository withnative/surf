# Understand the machinery inside meaningful work

## Keep literacy in service of capability

Supporting literacies help a person build, inspect, govern, recover, and transfer an
agentic-system capability. They are machinery, not the destination and not a prerequisite
course.

Begin with a meaningful outcome, observed strength, or system failure. Find the mechanism
that matters now, explain only enough to change what the person can see or do, apply the
distinction, and look for its consequences in a changed case.

Do not infer understanding from a familiar term, confident explanation, job title, or
technical register. Better evidence includes predicting a practical consequence, making
a suitable system choice, detecting a failure, or correcting a representation.

A literacy can recur at increasing depth. Managing one conversation’s context does not
demonstrate the ability to design context for several agents. Strong taste may precede
technical agent knowledge; technical fluency may coexist with weak operating capability.

The `shared-map-of-development` relates these literacies to `capabilities` and `builds`.
Consult the entries that illuminate the present work; there is no need to teach them all
or follow their numbering.

## 1. `models-and-agents`

**Enables:** Dependable levers, proportionate assurance, useful recovery, and adaptation
as models and harnesses change.

**Core distinctions:** A model generates probabilistic outputs from current inputs. An
agent adds instructions, tools, a loop, state access, and action boundaries. A harness can
retrieve context, call tools, preserve state, and enforce permissions, but none of these
makes output automatically true or action automatically appropriate.

Lead with practical consequences before architecture vocabulary:

- identical instructions may not produce identical results;
- tool availability differs from permission, successful use, or verification;
- agent confidence and verbal reasoning are not completion evidence;
- an apparent model failure may be missing context, a bad tool, an unreachable source,
  weak feedback, or a contradictory instruction; and
- frontier changes can retire previously useful constraints.

**Evidence of understanding:** The person locates a failure in the wider system rather
than reflexively rewriting the prompt; matches autonomy and checking to consequence; and
predicts what changes when tools, state, or feedback are removed.

**Can deepen from:** One response → a bounded tool loop → a persistent lever →
heterogeneous agents and tools → monitoring frontier change across a system.

## 2. `context-and-state`

**Enables:** Dependable levers, shared reality, collective action, recovery, and
trustworthy attention interfaces.

> Shared reality is durable and traceable. A context window is one agent’s temporary,
> selective projection of that picture.

Context is not simply “more information.” Help the person and system decide what must be
present now, what should persist, what has become stale, what conflicts, and what should
be deliberately discarded.

Core distinctions:

- **working context** — instructions, messages, tool results, retrieved sources, and
  temporary reasoning available to one attempt;
- **durable state** — goals, decisions, evidence, ownership, dependencies, standards,
  and unknowns intended to survive attempts;
- **retrieval** — selecting a relevant projection, not recreating the whole store;
- **compaction** — lossy compression that should preserve decisions, provenance,
  material uncertainty, and unresolved work;
- **fresh start** — discarding contaminated working context while carrying useful
  rulings forward explicitly; and
- **handoff** — transferring outcome, state, decisions, evidence, exclusions, and open
  questions across an agent, person, tool, or time boundary.

**Evidence of understanding:** The person can explain why a particular agent needs a
particular view; notice missing, stale, overloaded, or contaminated context; choose what
to retrieve, refresh, preserve, or discard; and leave a fresh agent able to orient without
bespoke briefing.

**Can deepen through the builds:**

1. In an `earned-lever`, assemble a coherent working projection and deliberately recover
   from drift, overload, or a missing source.
2. In a `shared-live-picture`, separate durable state from conversations and make a fresh
   agent retrieve, contribute, and update it.
3. In a `coordinated-initiative`, give different agents scoped views while preserving
   common decisions, dependencies, disagreement, and handoffs.
4. In a `principal-interface`, compress many working views into a traceable high-level
   picture without erasing uncertainty.
5. In a `system-improvement-build`, detect recurring context failures and revise
   structure, retrieval, freshness, or handoff practice.

**Failure check:** A large, current, shared context can still be false. Provenance,
contradiction, and challenge belong with state; volume and consensus are not assurance.

## 3. `standards-and-evaluation`

**Enables:** Standards and taste, dependable levers, assurance, compression, and honest
claims of improvement.

Core distinctions:

- explicit requirements versus tacit judgement;
- an exemplar versus a predicate or test;
- the generating context checking an objective signal versus endorsing its own judgement;
- a passing check versus residual uncertainty; and
- evidence of one output versus justified reliance across a class of work.

Use examples, comparisons, critique, counterexamples, and changed cases to make a quality
standard progressively learnable. State what would count as support, contradiction, or
an inconclusive result before using an outcome to change the system.

**Evidence of understanding:** The person chooses materially different evaluation for a
mechanical task, a judgement-heavy proposal, and a low-consequence exploration; names
what a passing check does not establish; notices when evaluation cost exceeds consequence;
and corrects a check that encodes the wrong quality bar.

**Can deepen from:** Inspect one output → evaluate a changed case → accumulate task-class
evidence → design assurance across several streams → measure a system-level intervention.

## 4. `delegation-and-control`

**Enables:** Direction, dependable levers, collective action, and assurance and governance.

Treat authority as a designed relationship, not a binary choice between manual control
and autonomy. Make explicit:

- what outcome or inquiry is delegated;
- which actions and information are available;
- where the agent must stop, ask, or escalate;
- which decisions remain with whom;
- what is reversible, consequential, or externally visible;
- who is affected and what visibility or correction rights they retain; and
- what evidence permits greater reliance next time.

**Evidence of understanding:** The person varies authority and checkpoints by consequence,
can explain why a boundary exists, detects when the human remains a hidden executor, and
revises the control structure from evidence rather than agent enthusiasm.

**Can deepen from:** One bounded run → a repeatable lever → several owners and handoffs →
delegated system maintenance with reserved mandate changes.

## 5. `coordination`

**Enables:** Shared reality and collective action without accidental collision or an
overloaded human courier.

Coordination joins responsibility, information, dependency, and integration:

- one owner or legible responsibility per decision or work product where ambiguity costs;
- explicit dependencies and what unblocks them;
- deliberate independent attempts kept separate until comparison;
- handoff receipts that carry decisions, exclusions, evidence, and unknowns;
- integration ownership and acceptance evidence; and
- a topology that can change when work changes.

**Evidence of understanding:** The person can predict where two streams will collide,
distinguish useful duplication from accidental duplication, design an integration path,
and show that added parallelism creates more useful throughput than coordination burden.

**Can deepen from:** Separate tasks → dependent streams → competing independent proposals
→ a temporary fleet → reorganisation around a changed constraint.

## 6. `information-compression`

**Enables:** Shared reality, operating through attention, handoffs, and grounded proactive
proposals.

Compression is an accountable transformation, not merely shorter prose. A useful layered
picture preserves direction, decisions required, meaningful progress, divergence,
uncertainty, exceptions, provenance, and a route to detail.

Useful questions include:

- Who is this view for, and what decision should it support?
- What was omitted, and could that omission reverse the conclusion?
- Which statements are observed, reported, inferred, or proposed?
- Can the reader trace a consequential claim to evidence?
- What changed since the last view?
- Which disagreement should remain visible rather than be synthesised away?

**Evidence of understanding:** The person makes a sound consequential decision from the
compressed view, finds the supporting detail when challenged, and identifies a plausible
case where the same compression would mislead.

**Can deepen from:** Handoff summary → shared-state view → multi-stream synthesis →
principal interface → proactive exception and improvement routing.

## 7. `system-learning`

**Enables:** Recovery, diagnostic compression, dependable levers, and system improvement.

Distinguish retaining more history from learning from evidence. Preserve:

- the observation and its provenance;
- the interpretation and important alternatives;
- the change made;
- the prediction stated before the run;
- the outcome, counterevidence, and uncertainty;
- the resulting rule, pattern, or retirement; and
- why the retained practice should be revisited as tools change.

**Evidence of understanding:** The person or system proposes a bounded change linked to a
recurring cost, states how its effect will be judged, records an honest verdict, and
avoids generalising beyond the evidence.

**Can deepen from:** Reflect on one failure → compare cases → earn a lever → improve a
topology or information flow → retire obsolete system practice.

## 8. `interaction-and-elicitation`

**Enables:** Direction, standards and taste, teaching, correction, and the interface
between human judgement and reusable system behaviour.

When the person cannot specify what they want, do not conclude that direction or quality
is unavailable. Choose an elicitation move suited to the evidence:

- ask for a concrete example when one exists;
- generate genuinely different alternatives when comparison is easier than specification;
- offer a deliberately provisional attempt when objection will reveal the standard;
- ask the system to restate intent or expose assumptions;
- separate clarification from a hidden intake form; and
- preserve corrections as evidence without turning every choice into a permanent preference.

**Evidence of understanding:** The person and system reduce consequential ambiguity,
transfer a stable part of the standard to a changed case, and surface rather than conceal
what remains tacit or novel.

**Can deepen from:** Clarify one request → elicit a quality standard → preserve repeated
judgement → let the system propose likely interpretations while novel ambiguity returns
to the person.

## A useful teaching pattern

When a literacy becomes relevant:

1. start with a meaningful outcome or observed failure;
2. expose the limiting mechanism in ordinary language;
3. explain the smallest useful distinction;
4. change or simulate one part of the system and practise the move;
5. look for the consequence in a changed case, missing condition, or realistic failure;
6. preserve only what will support future work; and
7. revisit at greater depth when scale or consequence makes it useful.

The `teaching-and-practice` moment guide governs delivery. Real execution remains outside
Surf’s learning practice: Surf can explain, map, simulate, guide practice, preserve
evidence, and review reusable system capability without taking over the immediate work.
